07. ANALYTICAL FINDINGS & STATISTICAL INTERPRETATION_

Deliveries and social status · Giancarlo Santoro

Research focus: how are social-status indicators associated with the dataset’s recorded online-food-ordering outcome?

[METHOD] What the evidence can support

The source contains 388 rows. After trimming text, standardizing occupation labels, removing the redundant target copy, and removing 103 exact duplicate substantive rows, 285 distinct records remain. The categorical tests and age analysis use all 285 records; model metrics use only the 57-record holdout. These are different analysis populations and must not be mixed.

The data dictionary does not establish that Output measures order frequency, expenditure, satisfaction, or future repeat ordering. Findings therefore refer explicitly to Output = Yes. Customer Type and Feedback are excluded from the predictive feature set to avoid potentially downstream information. The survey’s sampling frame and respondent identifiers are unavailable. Exact duplicate removal assumes matching substantive rows are repeat records, not distinct matching respondents.

Three omnibus categorical association tests use the Pearson χ² statistic with 9,999 shuffled outcome labels, seed 42, preserving observed margins. Cramér’s V summarizes association strength. Spearman’s age test permutes labels and uses an absolute statistic for a two-sided test. Monte Carlo p values use (exceedances + 1)/(9,999 + 1); Holm correction covers these four exploratory tests. Independence/exchangeability of rows is an assumption, not established by deduplication. All confidence intervals are descriptive 95% Wilson intervals, not population-representative guarantees.

Visualization

Analysis

The highest observed proportion is Single (160/189; 84.7%); the lowest is Prefer not to say (4/9; 44.4%), a descriptive gap of 40.2 percentage points. The omnibus Pearson statistic is 23.616; Cramér’s V = 0.288. Label-permutation p = 0.0001; Holm-adjusted p = 0.0004. This provides evidence of an association after correction at α = 0.05. The omnibus result does not establish which individual pairs differ.

Interpretation & research connection

This finding connects social status to the recorded Yes/No outcome, not to spending or order frequency. Age, occupation and income can be intertwined, so the unadjusted association cannot identify an independent or causal effect. Sparse groups and the dataset’s unknown sampling design limit generalization; intervals assume independent observations and are not simultaneous pairwise tests.

FINDING 4 / 5

[7.4] Age differences are cross-sectional, not time trends

Research question

Is age monotonically associated with Output = Yes?

Evidence

Observed ages span 18–33 years. Spearman ρ = -0.264; two-sided label-permutation p = 0.0001; Holm-adjusted p = 0.0004. The test uses exact ages, not the displayed bands.

Visualization

Analysis

The rank association is negative: older ages tend to have lower recorded Yes outcomes in this sample. The binary outcome creates many tied ranks. There is evidence against independence after the four-test Holm correction. Bars are 95% Wilson intervals under independent-observation assumptions.

Interpretation & research connection

These are different respondents observed in a cross-sectional dataset. Age is not a timestamp, so this is not evidence that an individual’s behavior changes over time. No date or repeated-order field is available to support trend, seasonality or forecasting claims.

Analysis

TabPFN classifies 43/43 actual Yes records correctly but only 1/14 actual No records. It predicts Yes for 56 of 57 records. Its 44/57 correct predictions exceed the all-Yes majority baseline (43/57; 75.4%) by only one record, or 1.75 percentage points. Its tied accuracy and F1 do not make it superior in ranking or minority-class detection. Gradient boosting has the highest observed AUC (0.786), while nearest centroid has the highest balanced accuracy (68.1%). These metrics answer different questions: ranking quality versus average class recall. No significance claim about model differences is made from this small reused holdout.

Interpretation & research connection

The newest model is not automatically the best model for the research task. The high F1 is for the majority Yes class; it must be interpreted alongside specificity, balanced accuracy and AUC. Similar input profiles and class imbalance plausibly contribute to the limited gains, but this experiment does not establish the cause. The current feature set provides limited evidence of useful No detection. Use fresh grouped validation and a task-specific error cost before selecting a model or changing its threshold.

[7.6] Synthesis for the research results section

  • Marital Status: Cramér’s V 0.288, Holm p 0.0004.
  • Monthly Income: Cramér’s V 0.229, Holm p 0.0051.
  • Occupation: Cramér’s V 0.275, Holm p 0.0008.

The observed social-status differences provide hypotheses about the recorded outcome; they do not establish that status determines behavior. The 20-model experiment demonstrates why predictive performance needs multiple metrics: TabPFN-3.5 ties the leading accuracy/F1 results while recognizing only one of fourteen No outcomes. A defensible conclusion separates in-sample associations from holdout prediction and avoids causal or time-trend claims.

Next research steps

Confirm the target definition, obtain respondent IDs and a documented sampling design, collect additional minority-class records, and validate on new grouped data. Adjusted association modeling and confidence intervals accounting for respondent clusters would strengthen the findings. Do not choose a threshold using the current test labels.
